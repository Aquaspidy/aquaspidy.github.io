Shot Quality (qSQ)

0-99 rating that reveals whether your offense is generating good looks — regardless of whether the shot goes in.

Voop's shot quality detector evaluates every scoring opportunity based on defender distance. When a player shoots, the system measures how far the closest defender stands, calculating an expected field goal percentage from 0-99. A wide-open three generates a 90+ qSQ, while a contested fadeaway might score below 20.

How It Works

📏 Defender Distance

Measures pixels between shooter and closest defender in real time. The closer the defender, the lower the qSQ.

🎯 Expected FG%

Converts distance data into a 0-99 score representing the probability of making that shot based on defensive pressure.

📊 Aggregation Engine

Averages qSQ across possessions to identify trends — is your offense actually generating good looks or just getting lucky?

🤖 YOLOv8 Detection

Powered by YOLOv8 object detection to identify player positions, bodies, and tracking IDs across the court.

Traditional Analytics vs. Voop qSQ

📋 Traditional Box Score

"Player went 2-for-10 from three" — no context about defensive pressure.

🎯 Voop qSQ

"Player generated 85+ qSQ on 8 of 10 attempts — great looks that just didn't fall."

In-Game Application

Unlike traditional analytics that only track makes and misses, Voop reveals whether your offense is generating good looks regardless of results. Coaches use this to identify players settling for bad shots versus those who consistently create advantages. The system aggregates qSQ across possessions, showing if poor shooting nights stem from bad luck (good looks that missed) or a broken offense (bad looks every time). This guides lineup adjustments and shot selection drills with objective data, not just shooting percentages.
