What It Does

Voop's 3D Shot Quality Analysis uses multi-camera triangulation to calculate real, physical shot metrics that go far beyond simple defender distance. The system determines actual release height (in meters), release angle (in degrees), and true 3D distance to the hoop — providing an unprecedented level of shot analysis.

Every shot attempt is assigned a 0-100 quality score based on a composite of these metrics: optimal release height (2.6m), ideal arc (55°), distance from hoop, and defender proximity. A wide-open three with ideal arc might score 90+, while a contested fadeaway from deep might score below 30.

Unlike traditional analytics that only track makes and misses, Voop reveals whether your offense is generating good looks regardless of results. Poor shooting nights might actually be bad luck (good looks that missed), or they might be a broken offense generating low-quality shots every time.

How It Works

Triangulation
Uses multi-camera setup to reconstruct 3D position of each player and the ball in real time.
Release Height
Calculates actual vertical position of the ball at release point. Higher release = harder to block.
Release Angle
Measures the arc of the shot. Optimal arc around 55° maximizes make probability.
Quality Score
Composite metric combining height, angle, distance, and defender proximity. Higher = better look.

In-Game Application

During games, coaches can see every shot's quality score in real time. If a player consistently takes 70+ quality shots but isn't making them, the problem is likely execution — not shot selection. If they're consistently taking sub-40 shots, the offense needs adjustment.

Post-game reports show shot quality distribution by player, lineup, and quarter. Coaches can identify which players are creating good looks versus settling, which lineups generate the best shot quality, and whether shot quality drops in the fourth quarter — indicating fatigue or defensive adjustment.

The 3D metrics also enable advanced scouting — opponents can be analyzed for shot quality tendencies, helping coaches design defenses that force them into low-quality attempts.
