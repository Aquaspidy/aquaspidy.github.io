What It Does

Voop's Heat Maps visualize player and team positioning intensity across the court. Using Gaussian smoothing and position tracking data, the system generates heat maps that show where players spend the most time and which areas of the court are most active.

Each player's position is tracked frame-by-frame, creating a density map of their movement. These individual maps are aggregated into team-level heat maps, revealing tactical patterns — whether a team operates primarily in the paint, on the perimeter, or in specific zones like the elbows or corners.

Heat maps are color-coded from cool (low activity) to hot (high activity), making it easy to spot trends at a glance. Coaches can see if their offense is getting to the paint enough, if their defense is spread too thin, or if a key player is operating in the right areas.

How It Works

📍 Position Tracking

Each player's (x, y) position is recorded every frame. Position data is stored in a history buffer for analysis.

📊 Gaussian Smoothing

Raw position data is smoothed using Gaussian filter (sigma=15) to create smooth, readable heat maps.

🎨 Color Mapping

Activity intensity is mapped from blue (low) → green → yellow → red (high) for immediate visual understanding.

📈 Per-Player Analysis

Individual heat maps for each player, plus team aggregation. Top players identified by activity count.

In-Game Application

During games, coaches can view live heat maps to see where their team is active and where opponents are exploiting them. If opponents are consistently scoring in the paint, the heat map will show high activity there — indicating a defensive breakdown.

Post-game heat maps reveal deeper patterns: which players are most active, which lineups create the best spacing, and whether offensive or defensive schemes are being executed as planned. For example, if a player is supposed to operate in the corners but their heat map shows them in the paint, coaches can adjust the game plan.

Heat maps also enable opponent scouting — analyzing an opponent's heat map reveals their tendencies and weaknesses, helping coaches design game plans that exploit specific areas of the court.
