Post-Game Coach Feedback

A quick, actionable two-paragraph memo after every game — before the next day's practice.

A detailed report after every game is not practical. But what you can do is write a 2-paragraph feedback memo to your coach.

Be Observational

After watching the game live or from replay, look for things the team did well — or did not do well — that were critical to the win or loss.

You can get help from apps like GameChanger. They have AI summaries that take box scores and game flow into consideration. Read those summaries and combine them with your own findings.

The Two-Paragraph Structure

Personally, I prefer two paragraphs:

Paragraph 1: What Went Well

Focus on strengths and positive performances that contributed to success.

Paragraph 2: What Needs Work

Focus on weaknesses and areas for improvement.

📜 Neutrality Still Applies

Keep in mind that the neutrality rule matters here as well. Don't criticize or compliment anyone by name. I never mention specific names in my feedback.

Focus on team-level observations and statistical trends, not individual performances.

Example Feedback

Paragraph 1 — What Went Well

"We dominated our opponents in rebounds today, grabbing 7 extra defensive rebounds and 6 extra offensive rebounds that resulted in 10 second-chance points. These critical rebounds allowed us to secure our lead and prevent our opponents from gaining momentum in the last 2 minutes."

Paragraph 2 — What Needs Work

"Turnovers were a huge issue for us today. Five players had more than 3 turnovers. Six of our turnovers turned into steals for our opponents, which resulted in 12 fastbreak points that blew our lead midway through the second half."

What Makes These Examples Effective

✅ Specific Numbers

"7 extra defensive rebounds" — not just "we rebounded well"

✅ Causal Connection

"resulted in 10 second-chance points" — links action to outcome

✅ Neutral Wording

"Five players had more than 3 turnovers" — factual, not blaming

✅ Game Context

"midway through the second half" — helps coach pinpoint timing

⏰ When to Send

Send one to the coaching staff after every game — and before the next day's practice. This gives coaches time to apply your insights into live action during practice.

Do's & Don'ts

✅ DO Include ❌ DON'T Include
Specific statistics (rebounds, turnovers, points) Player names or jersey numbers
Cause-and-effect relationships Emotional language ("awful", "amazing")
Game timing context (halftime, last 2 minutes) Comparisons between players
Actionable insights for practice Criticism or praise of individuals
