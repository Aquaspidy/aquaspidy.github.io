Keeping Live Scores

More than just numbers — live scoring is your window into the game's hidden patterns.

Ditch the Paper

If your team is still keeping scores on a paper spreadsheet, you should probably change methods. Paper spreadsheets are easy to mess up and always miss plenty of important stats like rebounds and assists. This becomes a problem later when you're trying to reflect on why you won or lost and conclude strengths and weaknesses. Numbers are powerful — they never lie — so you want to get as much out of a game as possible to know yourself thoroughly.

Digital Scorekeeping Apps

Use free, public digital scorekeeping apps like GameChanger (developed by Dick's) — or if you have a bit of budget, you can use Synergy. These apps are easy to use and easy to learn. All you need to do is tap on buttons, and they calculate all the needed stats automatically. GameChanger even generates an amazing game report concluding the strengths and weaknesses of both teams after each game.

📱 GameChanger (Free) 💎 Synergy (Paid)

Beyond Button-Pushing

As a statistician, simply sitting courtside and clicking buttons is not enough. You need to be analyzing the game in your brain all the time. Look for patterns, such as:

  • "Their guards always use that pump fake to draw fouls — all three of our center's fouls came from that."
  • "Only our point guard is moving all the time. Other players are stagnant, resulting in forced buzzer-beater airballs in four consecutive possessions."

Keep those thoughts in mind and tell your coach during timeouts or halftime.

💡 Gold Tip: Build a Relationship with Your Coach

Have a tight relationship with the coach. A good relationship opens up all kinds of opportunities you'll never imagine. You can do this by saying hi to them on campus whenever you see them, inviting them for a shoot-around after practice, or just sitting down on a weekend to talk basketball — or anything in life — with them.

Trust me, this tiny but extremely useful tip will pay off more than you expect.

The Real Value of Live Scoring

Keeping live scores may seem superfluous at first, but it actually does much more than just showing scores. It is an absolute beast during timeouts and halftime locker room talks. Show the coach the stats page on the app, and combine those stats with your observations from the game.

"Look coach, we only have 4 assists by halftime, and 3 of them are from just 1 player. From what I saw, other players are stagnant when one player drives toward the rim. We need to move around more — our opponents are having a relaxing day guarding us. We can't make it that easy."

"We are getting destroyed on rebounds. I know we might not be as tall as them, but I think this can be fixed by getting more steals. Their forwards move slow compared to ours."

Two pairs of eyes always work better than one. Never be ashamed or scared to express your own opinions, even if they're wrong. Nobody is born understanding basketball. Your coach is smart enough — they'll make the right decision from both of your perspectives. You will become an expert soon enough if you work hard to practice your skills at interpreting the game.

📺 Another Tip

Watch other basketball games — the NBA, college, even other high school games. Watch their tactics, and observe how each plan and counterplan is executed in response to actions on the court. It's both relaxing and beneficial for your basketball IQ.

After the Game

After the game, download the spreadsheet and send it to your coach. This data is also critical for the post-game feedback we'll cover in Step 5.
