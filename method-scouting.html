Opponent Scouting Reports

"Know your enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles."

— Sun Tzu

It is obviously important to know who you will be playing against, so it can be very helpful for your coach and team to have an overview of who you're playing against before the game.

Tools I Use

Prephoops Hudl MaxPreps RecruitTheBronx YouTube Instagram

Step 1: Get the Roster

Start with their roster, which you can find on their school website under the athletics page, or on Hudl, and sometimes MaxPreps. Now you have their roster, and it's time to dig deeper.

Step 2: Watch Game Footage

Look up their team on YouTube, Hudl, or Instagram, or research about their team on Prephoops to watch their game footages. Simply put down what you see on those videos — things like:

  • "#0 can shoot really well"
  • "#5 looks like he's 7 foot tall but looks really slow"
  • "They really like to run 2-3 zone defense, and it seems like this method gave their opponents plenty wide-open 3 point opportunities in the corner & on the top of the key"

If you don't have time, Hudl highlights is always a good option. Watch how their team offense operates, and put down notes.

Step 3: Organize Your Notes

Personally I like to separate my notes into a team section and a player section.

🏀 Team Section

Write about how you think they play as a team — things like man-to-man, zone defense, relies heavily on speed, centers around core player isolation, pick & roll, posting up then kick to corners, or 3-point heavy goes into this part. This section helps your coach create specific plays and tactics as a team so the team can familiarize similar situations in practice, simulating real-game environment.

👤 Player Section

The player section helps each individual player. They will know the features of who they will be guarding or who they will be guarded by, getting them mentally and physically prepared for the game. If you have some good ideas in your mind or saw their opponents in videos effectively guarding a player, you can even consider putting down guarding tips like:

  • "You can leave him open on perimeter, he mainly scores under the rim"
  • "Force him left, he always pulls up a reluctant fadeaway jumper when he goes left and I never saw him make one"

Step 4: Finalize & Send

Put these together as one single file. Personally, I don't like them to exceed 6 pages — otherwise people will get tired reading.

Send those notes to your coach, so he can develop counterplans against their playstyle.

⚠️ Ethical Reminder

Keep in mind that you should never use information in their profile meant for college recruiting for your own scout, which is not ethical. You can get into trouble if you misuse public information.
