Writing Periodic Recaps

The team's regular health checkup — reflecting on performance to extend win streaks or break slumps.

Periodic recaps are perhaps the most important step of being a good statistician. For a span of 5 to 9 games, your team might be on a win streak — or might not be performing as well as expected. It is critical to extend that win streak or stop that slump by doing some in-depth reflection. Think of it like a regular physical health checkup for the team.

The Framework

The game can be broken down into 2 main categories: offensive and defensive. Each then divides into team and individual. Each category will have strengths and weaknesses, and it is your job to put all of them together in one file.

Step 1: Talk to Your Coach

I recommend the first step to be talking to your coach. Go discuss your observations with them and see what they think. Then, with those insights in mind, you can start planning your entire file.

Ask yourself: What did we do well as a team? What still needs improvement?

📜 The Golden Rule: Stay Neutral

An important note here: you should never criticize or compliment a specific player in your report. The report must be factual and neutral. You are not the coach — that is not your job. The file's purpose is to present facts neutrally and explain what is happening, leaving the judgments to the coaching staff.

Personally, I never write strengths or weaknesses for individual players — only for the entire team.

Step 2: Plan Your Argument

Once you've determined what to write about for each category, you need to build your case with data.

For example, if you decide that over the past 7 games your team has been winning mainly due to offensive rebounds and second-chance points, a good idea would be to put a comparison graph of offensive rebound rates for winning games vs. losing games — or your team vs. opponent teams. Include similar comparison graphs of other less important factors together to emphasize the importance of offensive rebounds.

Generate those graphs using R code or other apps you prefer.

Two Writing Principles

1️⃣

Concise and Straightforward Language

Don't use fancy words when simple ones will do. This is about practicality, not a literature essay.

2️⃣

Be Comprehensive — Avoid Response Bias

Make sure you are as comprehensive as possible. Don't include one stat and leave other important ones out just to make a claim. That is called response bias in statistics.

Since you are trying to find associations with observational data, you want to exclude all potential confounding variables or important impactful factors. That's why planning matters so much before you write.

📤 After You Write

Send the report to your coach and discuss it with them. Ask for improvement advice — that's how you get better.

Bonus: If you're passionate, you can even present it to the whole team in a short presentation!

Example Report Structure

📋 Title: Periodic Recap — Games 12-18

📊 Key Finding: Offensive rebounding rate of 38% in wins vs. 22% in losses

🏀 Offensive Strength: Second-chance points (average 14 PPG in wins)

🛡️ Defensive Weakness: Transition defense after made baskets

📈 Supporting Graph: Four Factors comparison chart

✅ Recommendation: Crash offensive glass harder; send one player back on makes

Note: All observations are team-level, not player-specific
